The term Agentic GRC did not start here. Platforms are already selling it, and their definition is autonomy: agents that execute complete workflows on their own, with humans somewhere in the background providing "strategic oversight".

That definition fails on contact with a regulated environment. Autonomy demos well. It does not survive a second line review, an internal audit, or a conversation with a regulator about who approved what and on what evidence.

This site exists to hold a different definition, written from the practitioner's side: agent systems that perform risk and compliance work with the determinism, evidence, and accountability the work has always required. The [manifesto](/) is the founding document: seven principles, stated plainly, that reject the vendor definition and replace it with one a practitioner can actually defend.

A manifesto states what you believe. It does not, by itself, constrain a running system. So alongside it we are publishing [the standard](/standard/): the same seven principles rewritten as directive rules, meant to be pasted straight into a GRC agent's system prompt. It also carries the domain grounding an agent needs to operate inside those principles rather than just agree with them: the four functions, three lines of defence, known failure modes, the autonomy ladder. The manifesto is what we believe. The standard is what an agent is built to.

The writing that follows will be working systems, stated limitations, and no slideware.

If that standard sounds like your standard, you are in the right place.
